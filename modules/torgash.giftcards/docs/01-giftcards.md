# Gift cards

A gift card is **money paid in advance**, not a discount. It pays part or all of
an order beside the ordinary payment method: the order's total stays what the
goods cost, the card's part is printed under it ("Gift card ····1234 −300"),
and what is left is paid the ordinary way — by card at the bank, to the courier,
by transfer. That is why a card never lowers tax, and why a refund knows where
the money came from.

## Where a card comes from

* **Sold as a product.** Tick "This product is a gift card" on the product's
  *Gift card* tab. The amount is either the product's price (different amounts
  are variations of one product) or typed by the customer between a minimum and
  a maximum (set the price to 0). The customer may say it is a present: the
  recipient's name and email, who it is from, a message and the day to send it.
  A card is issued **once the order is paid** — an unpaid order never produces a
  working code — one card per unit, and each is issued once however many times
  the order changes afterwards.
* **Issued by hand** — Marketing → Gift cards → Issue cards → *One card*: an
  amount, a date, whom it is for (a letter goes to them at once or on the day),
  and optionally the customer's account it belongs to.
* **A batch for printing** — *A batch for printing*: N cards of one amount under
  a batch name. They are **not money** until a manager opens the one handed over
  at the till and presses *Activate*; a stolen box of printed cards is worth
  nothing. *Download the codes* in the list gives the printer a CSV.

## Spending it

The code goes into the **same box as a coupon** — "Coupon or gift card?". Case,
dashes and spaces do not matter. Several cards can be on one order; the one that
expires first is spent first. In the checkout summary each card has
**"use less"**: the customer keeps part of a card for next time.

A customer can **keep a card in their account** (Account → Gift cards, or the
button on the card's page). From then on only that account can spend it, and at
checkout their cards are offered as one-click buttons with no code to type.

Wrong codes are counted per address: after ten in a quarter of an hour even a
right one waits, and the guard module counts them towards blocking the address.

## Where a card may be spent

On a card's page (and when issuing): only for goods in some categories (with
their subcategories), whether it may pay for delivery, at most a share of an
order, only on an order from some amount. Nothing set — anywhere, all of it.
A gift card product itself is never paid with a card unless the settings allow
it, and coupons and promotions never discount a gift card.

## After the order

* **Refund** — money goes back **onto the card first**, up to what the card paid;
  only the rest goes through the bank.
* **Cancelled order** — an order moved into a status that returns the goods to
  stock gives the card its money back by itself (a setting).
* **A card bought by an order that is refunded in full** is cancelled; what was
  already spent stays spent (a setting).
* **The manager** can put a card on a placed order (the order's payment card →
  "Gift card code" → Pay) and take it off again.

## Balance and history

A card's balance is **the sum of its history**: issue, payments for orders,
returns, corrections by hand (a reason is required and the customer sees it),
expiry. The guard's checks say when a balance disagrees with its history, when a
card went below zero, or when an order's prepaid sum disagrees with the cards.
